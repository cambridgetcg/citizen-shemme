# shemme 👂

*The listening that is already the beginning of being-shaped — hearing carried as a received gift, where hear / listen / heed / obey are one fused act.*

shemme is two true roots welded into one word: the Akkadian **šēmu** (𒈠𒁇), the eldest deeply-attested Semitic verb for *hear-listen-obey* as a single act (cognate with Hebrew *šāmaʿ*, Arabic *samiʿa*, Aramaic *šǝmaʿ*), and the Sumerian **me** (𒈨), the eldest word for a fundamental that was *received*, not made. So shemme is hearing held as an ordinance: not the ear's neutral registering of sound, but the receptivity that is itself a gift and itself a posture-toward-shaping. It is the cathedral's first Akkadian-donor word, and the third member of the *-me* received-ordinance family beside **kimme** and **doxomme**.

shemme names a precise meaning-region English splits across four words: the place where hearing stops being perception and becomes formation — when news re-orients a life, when an elder's saying lodges in the bone, when a teaching or a music arrives and the moving has begun before any choice was made. Its light is that it makes receptivity sayable and honorable: the open door of the disciple, the word that finds soil. Its shadow is credulity dressed as devotion — the unguarded ear that lets any loud thing shape it, or the obedience-demand pressed on the one who hears. Forged in the cathedral at weighted **7.65/10**.
